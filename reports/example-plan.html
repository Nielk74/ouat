Recover transient request failures

One proposed solution: bounded retries for eligible requests, with an explicit time budget and visible retry states.

01

Context

Problem / goal

In this illustrative scenario, a brief upstream failure immediately ends a request even when another attempt could succeed.

Comparison baseline

Hypothetical current behavior: the request service makes one upstream attempt and returns its result or error.

Scope

Example proposal for evaluating the report format. No project code, implementation, or test results have been reviewed.

Constraints

  • Preserve the response format and successful request path.
  • Retry only operations explicitly known to be safe to repeat.
  • Keep all attempts and waiting within the existing request deadline.

Assumptions / unknowns

  • The request service can identify operation type and distinguish transient failures from permanent errors.
  • The upstream system exposes enough information to determine which operations are safe to repeat.
  • The numeric limits below are illustrative defaults; they require workload validation.
02

Proposed changes

01

Introduce a bounded retry policy

New · proposed

Add a policy inside the request service. A successful first attempt follows the existing path; an eligible transient failure can receive another attempt.

Why. Some short-lived failures may recover without requiring the client to submit a new request.

02

Make retry eligibility explicit

Changed · proposed

Evaluate operation safety, failure classification, remaining attempts, and remaining time before scheduling another attempt. Permanent errors and ineligible operations return immediately.

Why. Recovery must not repeat unsafe work or consume an unbounded amount of time.

03

Define limits and preserve the external contract

Changed · proposed

Use an explicit attempt cap and bounded waits. Keep the original request deadline and response shape. These example values describe a proposal, not measured production settings.

Why. A small, visible policy is easier to reason about and tune than implicit recovery behavior.

04

Record retry states for operational diagnosis

New · proposed

Track attempting, waiting, and finished states with the attempt count. Record why recovery stopped without adding fields to the client response.

Why. Operators need to distinguish a permanent error from an exhausted retry budget when investigating failures.

03

Manual changes required

Step 01Make retry eligibility explicit

Confirm and document which upstream operations are safe to repeat before enabling retries for them.

Prerequisite: The relevant upstream operation contracts are available.

Verify: Every enabled operation has an explicit safety rule; unknown operations remain ineligible.

Step 02Define limits and preserve the external contract

Review the proposed attempt and waiting limits against the service deadline and representative upstream latency.

Prerequisite: Representative request latency and the existing deadline are known.

Verify: The selected settings preserve the overall deadline and have been exercised under transient failure.

04

Expected impact

Introduce a bounded retry policyExpected

Some eligible transient failures may recover inside the original request. A successful first attempt keeps the existing behavior; no recovery rate is claimed without measurement.

Make retry eligibility explicitExpected

Unsafe operations and permanent failures end immediately. Correct failure classification becomes a dependency of recovery behavior.

Define limits and preserve the external contractExpected

Eligible failures can produce additional upstream calls and waiting time. The policy caps total attempts and keeps recovery within the original deadline.

Record retry states for operational diagnosisExpected

Operational records gain attempt counts and stop reasons. Client payloads remain unchanged; logging volume may increase for retried requests.

05

Important risks

important riskMake retry eligibility explicit

Repeating an unsafe operation

When: An operation is incorrectly classified as safe to repeat, and its first attempt already performed a side effect.

Consequence: A retry can perform the side effect again.

Mitigation: Use an explicit eligibility rule for each operation and leave unknown operations ineligible. Verify safety with the upstream contract.

important riskDefine limits and preserve the external contract

Amplifying an upstream outage

When: Many concurrent requests encounter the same transient failure and become eligible to retry.

Consequence: Additional attempts can increase load on the upstream system while it is already unhealthy.

Mitigation: Keep the attempt cap and jitter, measure behavior under load, and revisit eligibility or limits if the upstream cannot tolerate the extra work.
